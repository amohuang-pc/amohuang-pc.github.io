About

Before Foster, I spent close to four years in transfer pricing, first at Deloitte Taiwan and then in independent practice. The work was classification under scrutiny. For every study I had to decide which companies count as comparable under the arm's length principle, write down the reason for each call, and be ready to defend it in a tax audit years later.

I came to the MSIS program at the University of Washington to add the technical side: machine learning, experimentation, and systems design. What carries over from tax is a habit of documented judgment, and a preference for tools that support decisions rather than replace them. I am also exploring a community platform for cross-domain exchange, because moving between fields is how I learn best.

Work

Steel price forecasting

Side project, 2025 to present

In consulting meetings I watched steel executives price large orders from experience and memory. Nobody asked for a model, so I built one: an XGBoost forecaster for daily steel prices, with walk-forward validation, naive and seasonal-naive baselines, and SHAP explanations for every prediction. The point is not to replace veteran judgment. It is to give that judgment something to check against, and to make the model show its work the way I had to show mine in an audit file.

Comparables benchmarking for transfer pricing

Professional work, Deloitte Taiwan and independent practice

For each transfer pricing study I screened hundreds of candidate companies: read their business descriptions and financials, accepted or rejected each one against a documented rubric, and recorded a rationale for every decision. The results had to be consistent across analysts and hold up under tax authority review years later. Client work cannot be shared, but the craft is portable: turning messy corporate disclosures into consistent, reviewable classification decisions.

Rebuilding the steel forecaster into a public repository, and looking for research work on how humans and AI systems make decisions together.
