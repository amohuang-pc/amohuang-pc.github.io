About Me

Before Foster, I spent close to four years in transfer pricing, first at Deloitte Taiwan and then in independent practice. The work was classification under scrutiny. What carries over from tax is a habit of documented judgment, and a preference for tools that support decisions rather than replace them. Projects

Steel price forecasting

Side project, 2024 to present

During executive interviews for transfer pricing studies, I watched senior leaders at major steel producers price multi-million dollar orders from experience and memory. Nobody asked for a model, so I built one: a daily steel price forecaster comparing gradient boosting models (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) against naive and seasonal-naive baselines, with walk-forward validation over held-out months, direction accuracy alongside error metrics, and SHAP explanations for every prediction. The point is not to replace veteran judgment. It is to give that judgment something to check against, and to make the model show its work the way I had to show mine in an audit file.

Code and results on GitHub

Dataset citation detection

Kaggle competition, Make Data Count, 2025

A pipeline that finds dataset references in scientific papers and labels each as a primary or secondary citation. Text extraction handles messy PDF and XML with layered fallbacks (lxml, pdfminer, pypdf). Identifier matching covers DOI plus accession schemes such as GEO, SRA, ENA, ArrayExpress, and BioProject, with deliberately conservative rules: PDB codes, for example, only count when the surrounding text actually mentions the Protein Data Bank, trading recall for precision. Each hit is then classified from its context window using linguistic cues that separate data the authors generated from data they reused.
